## 2. Core Philosophy of Software Engineering

- **Problem-Solution Dynamic:** Any software product is ultimately about solving a problem with a targeted solution.
  - Greater demand leads to more powerful software built over time.
- **Demystifying the Role:** You do not need to be a genius to work in tech. Tasks are structured inputs and outputs (e.g., Jira tickets).
- **Engineering vs. Coding:** Software engineering means being an engineer—if you are strictly writing syntax, you are not truly engineering.
  - Engineering encompasses critical thinking, problem-solving, bug hunting, system monitoring, data structures, algorithms, and architectural design. Code writing is only one part of the story.

## 3. Industry Evolution & The Impact of AI

### Shift in the Tech Stack & Market Expectations

- **Historical Context (10+ years ago):** Legacy systems often lacked clean separation between frontend and backend. Specialized juniors/fresh grads could easily land internships or junior roles in specific frontend niches. Responsiveness used to be a standout feature; now it is expected by default.
- **Pre-AI vs. Present:** 6+ years ago, frontend engineers could specialize heavily in a single framework for years. Today, due to advancing AI capabilities, rigid specialization is less viable.
- **The "T-Shaped" Developer:** Juniors do not need to know everything, but they must be flexible, adaptable, and maintain a broad foundation with deep capability in key areas rather than being strictly limited.

### AI's Role in Development

- **Will AI replace developers?** Neither a strict "yes" nor a strict "no" is accurate:
  - AI replaces repetitive, replaceable tasks like boilerplate code generation.
  - AI raises the baseline: Everyone is pushed to start from a more advanced level.
  - Software Engineer + AI is the true answer. AI accelerates work, but requires a human to guide it, understand fundamentals, and spot AI mistakes.
- **Fundamental Knowledge is Key:** Syntax is easily handled by AI. The most critical skill today is reading, understanding, and evaluating AI output, which requires strong computer science fundamentals.

## 4. Junior Market & Job Hunting Strategy

### Job Market Realities

- **Higher Competition:** The market has far more candidates entering the field than before, making junior positions significantly harder to secure than in previous years.
- **Recruiter & Manager Mindset:** Managers do not run educational centers; they look for business value and team productivity.
  - Instead of asking *"Why won't they hire me?"*, ask *"Why SHOULD they hire me?"* Put yourself in the recruiter's or hiring manager's shoes.
  - This shift in perspective drives you to refine your GitHub, LinkedIn, and project portfolio.

### Building an Impactful Portfolio & CV

- **Project Depth Over Basic Apps:** Do not show up to interviews with just a simple CRUD application. Recruiters look for real-world engineering practices:
  - Is security considered? Is Git workflow evident? Are APIs integrated cleanly? Is there CI/CD setup?
  - Even a simple project that clearly demonstrates deep engineering understanding makes a huge impact.
- **Freelancing & Practical Experience:** Early freelance projects or internships—even lower-paying ones—help push your CV forward by demonstrating real experience.
- **LinkedIn Strategy (Quality vs. Quantity):** Focus on quality posts. Sharing one well-understood, interesting technical topic catches a recruiter's attention far better than spamming random content.
- **The Golden Rule of Visibility:** If you work in a company under a manager, stay visible. Keep your manager updated on your progress without overdoing it—both internally within the company and externally on platforms like LinkedIn.
- **Attention to Detail:** Small details on a CV can open or close doors; refine how you present your skills and accomplishments.

### Salary vs. Learning Trade-off

- Evaluate job offers based on patience and growth potential.
- If a salary is below market rate but you are learning valuable skills, continue. If you are neither earning well nor acquiring new knowledge/experience, it may be time to move on.

## 5. Interviews, Mindset & Soft Skills

### Dealing with Interview Rejections

- High technical strength does not guarantee an offer at every company. Hiring is like fitting a puzzle piece (matching candidate $X$ to role $Y$).
- Rejections are often driven by many factors; they should not lead to depression or switching majors.
- Avoid comparing yourself to others. It might take 10 to 20 interviews before getting accepted. Use rejections as feedback to evaluate what you can improve.

### Soft Skills & Communication

- When candidates share similar technical skill levels, soft skills become the deciding factor.
- **Communication:** Being technically proficient is not enough if you cannot articulate your thoughts clearly.
- **Professionalism & Body Language:** recruiters and interviewers look at body language, professional conduct, and how effectively you can deliver ideas in meetings.

## 6. Defining Seniority & Career Growth

- Seniority (Junior vs. Mid-level vs. Senior) is **not** defined by age or total years passed.
- **True Experience vs. Repetition:** Repeating the same routine for 5 years without growth is equivalent to 1 year of experience repeated 5 times.
- **Self-Honesty & Continuous Learning:** Fresh graduates are not seniors regardless of intelligence. Growth requires debating ideas, debugging complex errors, learning team dynamics, and navigating real-world constraints.
- **Self-Comparison:** Measure your progress against your past self rather than comparing your timeline to others.
- **Patience:** Career titles and financial growth come naturally over time—maintain a balanced pace and protect your mental well-being while building your professional presence.

---

## 7. Key Pitfalls & Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Unprofessional Behavior:** Never attempt to bypass your manager or lie on your CV or in interviews.
2. **Reactive Management:** Be proactive, not reactive. Do not wait for a system or project failure before taking action; anticipate and prevent potential issues.
3. **Over-Engineering:** Avoid building unnecessary complexity or features that were not requested. You are collaborating within a team, not working on a solo vanity project.
4. **Imposter Syndrome:** When entering high-level companies, never feel like you do not belong. If you didn't qualify, they would not have extended an offer.
