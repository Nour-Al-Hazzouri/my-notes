## The Reality of Post-Graduation

- Many people follow paths that do not resonate with them and fail to discover their true skills.
- Whether you are a fresh graduate or not, building a career is a step-by-step process. Securing a job is not an automatic or immediate result of graduating.

### Three Questions We'll Answer

*The same questions every fresh graduate asks*

- **How do I get my first job?** Applications, proof, networking, and interview readiness.
- **How do I stand out?** CV, LinkedIn, GitHub, projects, and personal brand.
- **What should I focus on in tech?** Career lanes, market signals, AI, FDE, cybersecurity, and practical skills.

## How to Get Your First Job

- Getting a job isn't just about applying and getting an interview. It's about everything you do during your university years (especially the third year) and after graduation to prepare for it.
- **Key Steps:**
  - Build projects, complete internships, volunteer, etc.
  - Start connecting, networking, and actively pursuing jobs during this period.
  - Attend events, connect with professionals, and attempt to meet with people, even if it's just for 1-2 months.
  - Focus on this path even if some opportunities are unpaid.
  - Utilize available tools—LinkedIn is a great resource. Optimize your profile, check for hackathons, events, and internships.

## How to Stand Out

- It's not just about having a CV, a LinkedIn profile, or a project. Everyone might be a Computer Science (CS) student, but each has a different skill set.
- **Self-Discovery First:** CS has many branches. You must discover what resonates with you, what you like, and what you want. The market is huge, so randomly seeking any job won't help. Finding a specific skill and improving it will put you among the top candidates.

## What Should I Focus on in Tech?

- Learning specific programming languages in university is not enough, especially with the current AI hype.
- Companies want graduates skilled in specific topics, particularly since new positions are emerging in the market. You need to conduct market research and find where you fit best.
- **Portfolio Before CV:** A portfolio plays a massive role. Having a solid portfolio (starting with a university project) is more important than just building a CV. Knowing programming languages isn't enough; HR needs to see what you've actually worked on.
- Your portfolio must always stay up to date, readable, and organized project by project, with labels indicating whether it was a university project and in what industry.

### The Tech Market is Wider Than You Think

*You Don't Have to Fit One Box. Tech careers are no longer only "sit and code all day"*

**Various Career Lanes:**

- Frontend, Backend, Full Stack, Data, AI / ML, Cybersecurity
- Cloud, DevOps, QA, Product, FDE, UX / UI

**Highlight: Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)**

- **FDE = Forward Deployed Engineer.** Think of an FDE as someone who is part consultant + part engineer + part AI expert.
- Instead of only writing code, they:
  - Meet with clients.
  - Understand the client's business problems.
  - Build AI solutions or AI workflows.
  - Help companies actually deploy AI successfully.
  - Act as a bridge between business needs and technical execution.
- This role is perfect for people who don't want to be purely technical.
- **Key Takeaway:** Have one specialization in mind and take it from there. You cannot make a single CV for everything.

## Crafting Your CV

- Many people struggle to create a CV, thinking it should be a heavily designed template (like those on Canva)—which should **never** be done, especially for tech applications.
- If applying to a tech company, Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) won't read heavily designed CVs. It must be simple.
- **Structure of a CV:**
**Main Template used:** [[Moustafa Kader - CV Example.pdf]]
  - **Header:** Name in the middle, followed by the role you are targeting.
  - **Location:** If you reside in another city, do not mention that you are far away (for compensation reasons).
  - **Contact Info:** Professional email and a hyperlinked LinkedIn profile. You should also include a link to your portfolio.
  - **Objective Statement:** Briefly answers "Who am I?" and "What value can I provide to the company?" It should be simple and focus on what value you add, not why you want to join the company.
  - **Career Objective:** Optional but recommended.
  - **Profile Summary:** The first line states who you are and your experience, followed by your focus.
  - **Key Competencies:** CVs are no longer just lists of information and experiences. Competencies highlight your added value to the market. **Never** include skills you haven't mastered.
  - *Note:* These sections can be tailored based on the individual and the company being applied to.
  - **Professional Experience & Projects:** Usually, professional experience (like internships) is listed before projects. However, projects can be listed first if they are richer in content.
  - **Writing Professional Experience:** Be specific, use ATS-friendly keywords, and highlight accomplishments. Verbs should be in the past tense. ATS systems now look closely at dates (day, month, year) and may filter out CVs lacking detail.
  - **Technical Toolkit:** List the specific languages you master more than others and what makes your skill set unique.

## LinkedIn Optimization

- Everyone should have a LinkedIn profile, even high school or university students.
- **Banner & Profile Picture:** The banner reflects who you are (can be made on Canva) and must be professional. The profile picture must also be professional.
- **Name:** Use your real name, not a nickname.
- **Bio (Headline):** State who you are today (e.g., Full Stack Developer).
- **Details:** Fill in your current position, school, country, and all relevant details.
- **Open to Work:** Adding the "Open to Work" frame is optional, but it raises your chances if you are looking for a job. Fill in the specific job titles you are looking for after checking market demands on LinkedIn.
- **About Section:** Should answer: Who are you? What are your capabilities? What have you worked on? What are your ambitions? Include a call to action (e.g., "Happy to connect!").
- **Services & Career Breaks:** Mention services you provide. If you have career gaps (e.g., stopping work for a year), explain them constructively (e.g., getting a scholarship, taking courses, doing internships), even if the gap was due to a medical condition.
- **Education:** Only include your university education; nothing else is necessary.
- **Certifications:** Bootcamps and courses should be listed under Licenses and Certifications.
- **Skills:** Add any relevant skills to increase your visibility.
- **Recommendations:** Very important, especially from people you've worked with. It makes you appear more credible to recruiters (appears 3x more). Ensure recommendations are from people who genuinely know or have worked with you.
- **Job Hunting on LinkedIn:**
  - When searching for a job title, ensure you are applying to specific, recent openings (not months old).
  - Check posts, not just the jobs section, for interesting opportunities.
  - Always verify the person posting the job, as some can be fake or spam.
  - *Note:* LinkedIn records applications even if no CV is uploaded, so apply anyway.

## Q&A Session

**Q: Does the first year shape a student's work life?**

- **A:** The first year does shape a student's work life, and they should know the major they are in. However, it's not wrong to change positions within the same major later on.

**Q: How do you tailor a CV based on each company?**

- **A:** It involves research. Think like a recruiter and analyze the Job Description (JD) to understand exactly what they are focusing on.

**Q: Does being an associate's degree student differ from a bachelor's student if ATS filters CVs based on small details?**

- **A:** The degree itself doesn't matter as much. What matters are up-to-date certifications and mentioning which skills you acquired from where. Remove unrelated experience.

**Q: Are all remote job opportunities trusted?**

- **A:** You need to be cautious. Sometimes company websites are hacked, and many people suffer from scams. Always verify.
